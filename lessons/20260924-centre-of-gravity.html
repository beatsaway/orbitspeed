Centre of gravity

Science · Rocket stability · 30 min

Objectives Outcomes Do now I do We do You do Close Kit

Learning objectives

  • Say what the centre of gravity is, and what the centre of pressure is.
  • Explain that a rocket stays straight when the centre of gravity sits closer to the nose than the centre of pressure.

Learning outcomes

  • Balance the forks and post both definitions.
  • Play Qinglan Launch and submit the form, with a screenshot of the result.
  • Take a side in the closing debate.
6 min

Do now

Teacher

Two bottles, two forks, and one toothpick per pair. Ask: “How do you balance the forks with a toothpick on the bottle?” Do not give the method. Walk and listen.

Students

Balance the forks on the toothpick, and the toothpick on the rim. Talk about where the weight seems to sit.

Check

A pair can show a balance and say the weight is not at the toothpick tip. The name comes later.

4 min

I do

Factual

  • What is the centre of gravity?

Teacher

The centre of gravity is the point where the weight is centred. On the forks, that point hangs below the rim, so they stay put. Post: “What is the centre of gravity?”

Students

Watch one successful balance. Comment from the forks, not from a copied definition.

Check

Read three posts. Accept “where the weight balances”, not “where the air pushes”.

5 min

We do

Factual

  • What is the centre of pressure?

Conceptual

  • Why does a rocket stay straight only when the centre of gravity is closer to the nose than the centre of pressure?

Teacher

Open Qinglan Launch and point to the two dots. Magenta is weight. Cyan is the air’s push. Post: “What is the centre of pressure?” Ask which dot must sit closer to the nose.

Students

Answer the second post. In pairs, say which dot is closer to the nose on a rocket that should fly straight.

Check

Cold-call: centre of pressure is the air’s push, not the weight. Magenta sits closer to the nose than cyan.

12 min

You do

Teacher

Send students to Qinglan Launch. Leave the form open. If time runs out before a company ending, they screenshot the result on screen.

Students

  1. Play the rocket.
  2. Complete the form.
  3. Upload a screenshot of the result they reached.

Listen for

Form, 100 points.

  1. Centre of gravity: the point where the rocket’s weight is centred. (20)
  2. Centre of pressure: the point where the air’s push is centred.
  3. What stabilises a rocket: centre of gravity above centre of pressure.
  4. Lower the centre of gravity: payload in a lower position.
  5. Lower the centre of pressure: fins down and bigger. Retitle this item before class if the form still says “centre of gravity”.
  6. Screenshot of the end result.
3 min

Close

Debatable

  • Is a more stable rocket always a better rocket?

Teacher

Take one agree and one disagree. Do not close it with a single right answer.

Students

Vote, then one sentence each way. Use a tumble, a wobble, or a rocket that turned off course.

Check

An agree names a crash from a bad balance. A disagree names a rocket so stable it flew a curve instead of straight up.

Kit

  • Two metal forks, one wooden toothpick, and one bottle per pair
  • Google Classroom for the two posts
  • Qinglan Launch
  • Google Form “Centre of Gravity & Centre of Pressure”
