Rover and gravity

Science · Forces · 40 min

Objectives Outcomes Retrieval SkyLift Sandbox Researcher Design Exit IB Kit

Learning objectives

  • State the relationship between weight and mass.
  • Describe how the weight of an object changes with altitude, and how gravity changes with the mass of a planet.
  • Use gravity to suggest one improvement to a rover.

Learning outcomes

  • Submit the September retrieval quiz.
  • From SkyLift, say how weight and mass are linked, and how they are different.
  • From Gravity Sandbox, say what gravity has to do with a planet’s mass, and with distance.
  • Name one way gravity changes a rover design.
  • Submit “Gravity, Weight and Mass”. A Gravity Researcher screenshot is optional.

Questions

  2. How does heat transfer by conduction, convection, and radiation?
  3. How does aluminium foil help insulate heat in space?
  4. Why is remote-controlling a Mars rover from Earth a challenge?

Teacher

The slide says “Please finish the quiz”. The words September retrieval quiz are the link. Do not reteach. If they finish early, they comment on the three Google Classroom posts: what they already know about gravity, the link between weight and mass, and how gravity can help a rover design.

Students

Answer all five from the previous lessons. Silent. Hand the quiz in.

Listen for

  1. It causes metal parts to expand and contract.
  2. Conduction through direct contact. Convection by moving fluids. Radiation by electromagnetic waves through empty space.
  3. It reflects thermal radiation.
  4. Radio signals have a delay of more than 20 minutes.
  5. All of the above: temperature or radiation sensors, a camera or LiDAR, and chemical or mineral sensors.
8 min

SkyLift · weight and mass

Questions

  • What is the link between weight and mass?
  • How are they different?

Teacher

Send the class to SkyLift. Each brick is 2 kg. They try on the ground, then higher up. Leave the two questions on the slide. Do not give them the finished answer first.

Students

  1. Hold bricks on the ground.
  2. Go higher and try again.
  3. Answer the two questions.

Check

Mass stays 2 kg. Weight is the pull, and it gets smaller higher up. Do not accept “the bricks lost mass”.

6 min

Teacher Talk · rover design

Four short slides, about 90 seconds each. Say the three lines, then move on.

Less weight, less grip

  • On Mars the rover weighs less.
  • The wheels press down less, so they can slip.
  • Cleats dig into the sand so the wheels can grip.

The rocket lifts the mass

  • Mass is how much stuff is in the rover.
  • The rocket has to lift that mass off Earth.
  • A heavier rover is harder to launch. The picture shows a heavy rover still on the pad, and a light one already in the sky.

Six wheels share the push

  • Sand can slip under one wheel.
  • All six wheels drive, so they push together.
  • If one wheel sticks, the others can still move the rover.

Do not let it tip

  • On Mars the pull downwards is weaker.
  • A wheel that lifts off is harder to put back down.
  • One wheel climbs the rock. The others stay on the ground.
6 min

Exit ticket

Gravity, Weight and Mass. The Gravity Researcher screenshot is optional. Submit before they leave.

Questions

  1. Space has no gravity.
  2. Your mass is the amount of matter that you are made of.
  3. You have gravitational pull on other objects.
  4. The further away you are from a black hole…
  5. The further away you are from planet Earth…
  6. It is easier to escape a black hole…
  7. The more massive you are…
  8. Your weight changes on different planets.
  9. Your mass changes on different planets.
  10. You can lose weight by going up the mountain.

Teacher

They should already have the screenshot from Gravity Researcher. If someone says space has no gravity, send them back to the SkyLift result: the bricks still had weight, it was just smaller.

Listen for

  1. False. Gravity is weaker farther away. It does not switch off.
  2. True.
  3. True. Mass pulls on other mass.
  4. The weaker its gravitational pull on you.
  5. The weaker its gravitational pull on you. This is the SkyLift result.
  6. If you move away from it quickly.
  7. The more gravitational attraction you act on other objects around you.
  8. True. Weight = mass × g, and g is different on each planet.
  9. False. Mass stays the same.
  10. True. Higher up, the pull is weaker, so weight is smaller. Mass is unchanged.

Possible IB links

MYP key concepts

Change, relationships, systems, models. This lesson uses relationships: weight and mass, planet mass and gravity, and distance and gravity. g = GM / r² is the model. The rover-design talk comes after the games.

Related concepts

Forces, energy, adaptation, stability, innovation. Weight is the force. The retrieval uses energy transfer. The rover is adapted so it stays stable under the gravity of that world.

Global context

Scientific and technical innovation. Orientation in space and time: altitude above Earth, and the minutes a signal takes to reach Mars.

ATL skills

Research, critical thinking, transfer, collaboration. Retrieval is transfer from earlier lessons. The games are where they test a relationship before they write it.

Learner profile

Inquirers, thinkers, risk-takers, knowledgeable. SkyLift and the sandbox are the inquiry. Playing a mission and committing a number is the risk-taking.
