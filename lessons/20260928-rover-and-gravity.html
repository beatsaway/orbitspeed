Rover and gravity

Science · Forces · 41 min

Objectives Outcomes Retrieval Gravity SkyLift Investigate Design Exit IB Kit

Learning objectives

  • Say what gravity is, and what mass is.
  • State the relationship between weight and mass, and how we measure weight.
  • Describe how gravity changes with the mass of a planet, and with distance.
  • Use gravity to suggest one improvement to a rover, and say whether a Mars rover would work well on the Moon.

Learning outcomes

  • Submit the September retrieval quiz, then comment on the three Google Classroom posts.
  • From SkyLift, say what weight is, how we measure it, how it links to mass, and how they are different.
  • From Gravity Sandbox and Gravity Researcher, say what gravity has to do with a planet’s mass, and with distance.
  • Name one way gravity changes a rover design.
  • Submit “Gravity, Weight and Mass”, including the debate about a Mars rover on the Moon. A screenshot is optional.

Teacher Talk · gravity and mass

Three lines. Each one starts as “Gravity is …”, “Mass is …”, and “We measure mass in …”. Click to show the rest. Click again to hide it.

Reveal

  • Gravity is a force of attraction. It pulls masses towards each other.
  • Mass is the amount of matter in an object.
  • We measure mass in kilograms. It stays the same.
8 min

SkyLift · weight and mass

The slide says: open SkyLift in Google Classwork to investigate the following. Click a question to show the answer. Click again to hide it.

Questions

  • What is weight? Weight is how hard gravity pulls on a mass.
  • How do we measure weight? Use a balance, a scale, or a hook on a spring. The picture shows a big rock and a small rock on a balance, a dial scale, and a hook.
  • What is the link between weight and mass? More mass means more weight, in the same place.
  • How are they different? Mass stays the same. Weight can change.

Teacher

Each brick in the game is 2 kg. They try on the ground, then higher up. Do not give them the finished answer first.

Students

  1. Hold bricks on the ground.
  2. Go higher and try again.
  3. Answer the four questions.

Check

Mass stays the same. Weight is the pull, and it gets smaller higher up. Do not accept “the bricks lost mass”.

4 min

Teacher Talk · rover design

Four short slides, 1 minute each. Each one asks “How does gravity affect our rover design?” Click to show the short answer. Then say the three lines and move on.

The rocket lifts the mass

The rocket has to lift the rover’s mass. More mass is harder to launch. The picture is a rocket launching, and a rocket falling and exploding.

  • Mass is how much stuff is in the rover.
  • The rocket has to lift that mass off Earth.
  • A heavier rover is harder to launch.

Less weight, less grip

The rover weighs less, so the wheels can slip. Cleats help them grip.

  • On Mars the rover weighs less.
  • The wheels press down less, so they can slip.
  • Cleats dig into the sand so the wheels can grip.

Six wheels share the push

Six wheels push together, so one stuck wheel does not stop the rover.

  • Sand can slip under one wheel.
  • All six wheels drive, so they push together.
  • If one wheel sticks, the others can still move the rover.

Do not let it tip

A weaker pull makes a lifted wheel harder to put back down.

  • On Mars the pull downwards is weaker.
  • A wheel that lifts off is harder to put back down.
  • One wheel climbs the rock. The others stay on the ground.
6 min

Exit ticket

Gravity, Weight and Mass. The Gravity Researcher screenshot is optional. Submit before they leave.

Questions

  1. Space has no gravity.
  2. Your mass is the amount of matter that you are made of.
  3. You have gravitational pull on other objects.
  4. The further away you are from a black hole…
  5. The further away you are from planet Earth…
  6. It is easier to escape a black hole…
  7. The more massive you are…
  8. Your weight changes on different planets.
  9. Your mass changes on different planets.
  10. You can lose weight by going up the mountain.
  11. Do you think Mars rovers would work well on the Moon as well? Why?

Teacher

They should already have the screenshot from Gravity Researcher. If someone says space has no gravity, send them back to the SkyLift result: the bricks still had weight, it was just smaller. The last question is a debate. Accept either side if the reason uses gravity.

Listen for

  1. False. Gravity is weaker farther away. It does not switch off.
  2. True.
  3. True. Mass pulls on other mass.
  4. The weaker its gravitational pull on you.
  5. The weaker its gravitational pull on you. This is the SkyLift result.
  6. If you move away from it quickly.
  7. The more gravitational attraction you act on other objects around you.
  8. True. Weight = mass × g, and g is different on each planet.
  9. False. Mass stays the same.
  10. True. Higher up, the pull is weaker, so weight is smaller. Mass is unchanged.
  11. The Moon’s pull is weaker than Mars, so the same rover weighs even less. Less weight means less grip, so the wheels may slip more. The six wheels and the rocker could still help. It was built for Mars, so it may need changes for the Moon. Mass stays the same.

Possible IB links

MYP key concepts

Change, relationships, systems, models. This lesson uses relationships: gravity and mass, weight and mass, planet mass and gravity, and distance and gravity. g = GM / r² is the model. The rover-design talk comes after the games. The exit debate asks if a Mars rover would work on the Moon, where the pull is weaker.

Related concepts

Forces, energy, adaptation, stability, innovation. Weight is the force. The retrieval uses energy transfer. The rover is adapted so it stays stable under the gravity of that world.

Global context

Scientific and technical innovation. Orientation in space and time: altitude above Earth, and the minutes a signal takes to reach Mars.

ATL skills

Research, critical thinking, transfer, collaboration. Retrieval is transfer from earlier lessons. The games are where they test a relationship before they write it.

Learner profile

Inquirers, thinkers, risk-takers, knowledgeable. SkyLift and the sandbox are the inquiry. Playing a mission and committing a number is the risk-taking.
